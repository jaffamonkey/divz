Restore Britain is a fictionalised satirical depiction of a "political boutique" and elite lifeboat project founded in 2024 by Rupert Lowe, the MP for Great Yarmouth and unofficial patron saint of the "I’m not prejudiced, but..." demographic. The party positions itself as the "pure" alternative to Reform UK, catering specifically to a fanbase that finds Nigel Farage suspiciously cosmopolitan, the 21st century a bureaucratic overreach, and the phrase "human resources" a chilling communist experiment.

The party is best known for demanding the restoration of a Britain which, according to party literature, existed sometime between the invention of the steam engine and the first time a coffee was described as "flat". Scholars have described its programme as "a vibes-based constitutional panic" and "the sound a blazer makes when it learns about equality legislation".[1]

History [edit]

The party was conceived when Rupert Lowe realized that being a Reform UK MP required him to occasionally touch the hands of the general public. Seeking a more "distanced" form of populism, he launched Restore Britain as a vehicle for those who believe the United Kingdom should be run with the same ruthless efficiency as a private boarding school, a liquidated football club, or a WhatsApp group where everyone is an administrator.

Early meetings reportedly took place in rooms described by attendees as "panelled", "difficult to heat", and "containing at least one portrait of a man who definitely opposed decimalisation". The founding conference, held under the working title Britain: Has Anyone Tried Turning It Off and On Again?, concluded with the unanimous adoption of three core principles: restore order, restore pride, and restore the small plate of biscuits that had been removed during the coffee break.[2]

Founding myth

Party lore states that Lowe was visited in a dream by the spirit of a county cricket pavilion, which instructed him to gather the disaffected, the over-leveraged, and those who still write letters to the council in fountain pen. The accuracy of this account has been disputed by historians, several of whom note that the pavilion in question had been converted into luxury flats in 2017.
